Understand the science

Why does Earth rotate?

Earth inherited its rotation from the cloud of material that formed the Solar System billions of years ago. Because angular momentum is conserved, our planet continues to spin.

What if Earth stopped suddenly?

Objects at the equator currently move eastward at approximately 1,674 kilometres per hour due to Earth's rotation. If the solid Earth suddenly stopped, the atmosphere, oceans and unattached objects would initially retain much of their motion. The consequences would be catastrophic.

A gradual slowdown would be very different. The length of the solar day, atmospheric circulation and ocean distribution could change over time.

Would one day last an entire year?

If Earth stopped rotating relative to distant stars but continued orbiting the Sun, the Sun's apparent direction would still change over the course of the year. In this idealized model, a complete solar day would take approximately one orbital year.

How is rotation speed calculated?

v = 2πR / T

Here, v is the equatorial speed, R is Earth's equatorial radius and T is its rotation period. A shorter rotation period means a greater equatorial speed.

Frequently asked questions

Is Earth currently slowing down?

Over long periods, tidal interactions with the Moon tend to slow Earth's rotation. Short-term changes can also occur because of processes involving the atmosphere, oceans and Earth's interior.

Would gravity disappear if Earth stopped spinning?

No. Earth's gravitational attraction primarily comes from its mass. Stopping rotation would remove the centrifugal contribution that slightly reduces apparent weight, especially at the equator.

What if Earth rotated four times faster?

In this simplified model, Earth's rotation period would be approximately six hours. The centrifugal effect would increase, and a real planet's shape, weather and ocean circulation could also change.

Why are the solar day and rotation period different?

Earth moves along its orbit while rotating. It therefore needs to rotate slightly more than one full turn relative to distant stars for the Sun to return to the same position in the sky.
